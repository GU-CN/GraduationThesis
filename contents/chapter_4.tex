\chapter{Final design and implementation}\label{chap:final-design}

\section{Final design}

\subsection{Design evolution from the DR3 baseline}

The final implementation retains the modular system architecture established during Design Review~3 (DR3), but updates the learned policy and the demonstrated task scope. The DR3 design used task-specific Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT) checkpoints as the deployable baseline and treated language-conditioned bimanual control as future work. During subsequent development, the policy was replaced by the LeRobot implementation of $\pi_{0.5}$, and the workcell was extended to coordinated operation with two Franka Research~3 (FR3) robot arms. ACT is therefore retained in this thesis as an important development baseline rather than the final deployed controller.

The resulting system is a language-conditioned bimanual manipulation platform. It combines a dual-FR3 workcell, GELLO-based demonstration collection, synchronized multi-view visual observations, robot and gripper state acquisition, a LeRobot-compatible dataset, a fine-tuned $\pi_{0.5}$ policy, and robot-side execution and safety interfaces. The $\pi_{0.5}$ model is a vision-language-action policy designed to combine visual observations, natural-language task descriptions, robot state, and action generation~\cite{pi05}. The implementation used in this project follows the policy integration provided by LeRobot, which adapts the open-source $\pi_{0.5}$ implementation for the LeRobot training and deployment workflow~\cite{lerobot,lerobotpi05,openpi}.

\subsection{Overall system architecture}

The complete system is organized as a closed perception--language--action loop. At the beginning of an episode, the two FR3 arms and the task objects are returned to a documented initial condition. The camera subsystem provides complementary global and local views of the shared workspace, while the robot interfaces provide synchronized arm and gripper states. A natural-language instruction identifies the required task and is supplied to the policy together with the current visual and proprioceptive observations. The policy predicts robot actions, which are decoded by the LeRobot and robot-side interfaces and executed subject to the FR3 controller protections, joint limits, workspace limits, and emergency-stop procedure~\cite{frankafci}.

For a bimanual observation at time \(t\), the policy interface can be summarized as
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{a}_{t:t+H-1}
=
\pi_{\theta}
\left(
\mathbf{o}^{\mathrm{visual}}_t,
\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{left}}_t,
\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{right}}_t,
\ell
\right),
\label{eq:pi05-bimanual-policy}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathbf{o}^{\mathrm{visual}}_t\) denotes the synchronized camera observations, \(\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{left}}_t\) and \(\mathbf{s}^{\mathrm{right}}_t\) denote the two arm and gripper states, \(\ell\) is the natural-language instruction, and \(\mathbf{a}_{t:t+H-1}\) is the predicted bimanual action sequence. This interface preserves a separation between the learned policy and the deterministic robot-side safety layer.

\section{Design details}

\subsection{Dual-arm robotic workcell}

The mechanical platform consists of two FR3 robot arms operating in a shared, bounded workspace. Each arm provides seven degrees of freedom and retains the low-level protection mechanisms of the Franka control system. The dual-arm arrangement allows a task to combine workspace preparation, object stabilization, grasping, transport, alignment, insertion, and placement. The exact division of work between the left and right arms is learned from the demonstrations rather than prescribed as a fixed symbolic sequence.

Task-compatible grippers and fixtures are used to make the three demonstrations repeatable while preserving the contact-rich character of the operations. The power-socket fixture remains electrically unpowered during development and validation. Before an autonomous run, the operator verifies the fixtures, camera streams, arm states, gripper states, communication links, workspace clearance, and emergency-stop access.

\subsection{Demonstration collection and LeRobot dataset}

The data-collection path follows the DR3 design. GELLO leader devices provide an intuitive interface for recording robot demonstrations~\cite{gello}. During collection, the system records synchronized multi-view RGB observations, the measured state of both robot arms, both gripper states, and the commanded actions. The task instruction is stored with each episode so that the policy can associate language with the corresponding visual and action trajectory.

The accepted episodes are converted to the LeRobot dataset representation. Episode-level screening removes incomplete demonstrations and checks that the required observation streams, state dimensions, action dimensions, task instruction, and video records are present. Splitting data by complete episode prevents neighboring frames from one demonstration from being distributed across both training and validation sets. Dataset normalization and the mapping between camera names, state features, action features, and the two robot arms are stored as part of the policy configuration.

\subsection{LeRobot $\pi_{0.5}$ policy}

The final controller uses $\pi_{0.5}$ rather than the ACT baseline described in DR3. $\pi_{0.5}$ extends the $\pi_0$ family with heterogeneous co-training and semantic knowledge transfer intended to improve generalization across tasks, objects, and environments~\cite{pi0,pi05}. In the present project, the model is adapted through LeRobot to the dual-FR3 observation and action interface. The language instruction distinguishes the requested task, while the visual observations and robot states provide the current physical context.

The use of a VLA policy changes the role of language from a report-level task label to an input to the deployed controller. However, the model output is not sent directly to the actuators without checks. The robot-side interface validates the action representation and enforces the admissible command range before issuing commands to the two FR3 controllers. Missing observations, stale state, non-finite actions, communication timeout, or a command outside the configured limits causes the execution loop to stop.

\subsection{Safety and termination conditions}

The policy is deployed only in a bounded laboratory workspace. A run is terminated when the task completion condition is reached, the configured time limit is exceeded, an observation or communication failure occurs, a robot protective stop is triggered, manual intervention becomes necessary, or the safety observer activates the emergency stop. A collision, dropped component, incomplete insertion or placement, movement outside the workspace, protective stop, or manual assistance is recorded as a failed trial rather than a successful recovery.

\section{Implementation and integration}

\subsection{Implementation workflow}

Implementation proceeded through five linked stages. First, the dual-FR3 workcell, grippers, fixtures, cameras, and safety controls were commissioned. Second, the GELLO interfaces and robot bridges were used to record bimanual demonstrations for the three language instructions. Third, the recorded episodes were checked and converted into the LeRobot representation. Fourth, the $\pi_{0.5}$ policy was adapted to the project observation, language, state, and action interfaces. Finally, policy inference was connected to the two robot controllers and tested through dry runs before physical execution was enabled.

The integration sequence for each deployment is: verify the workcell and sensors; load the frozen policy and dataset statistics; confirm the two-arm feature mapping; provide the task instruction; run inference without robot motion; inspect the initial commands; enable the robot-side executor; and conduct the counted physical trial. This sequence makes interface errors easier to distinguish from policy errors and preserves a reproducible path from demonstrations to deployment.

\subsection{Current implementation status}

The current implementation uses the LeRobot $\pi_{0.5}$ policy with two FR3 arms and has completed the end-to-end integration path for the ring-over-can, plug-in-socket, and memory-module-removal tasks. This establishes that the language input, visual observations, bimanual state and action interfaces, policy inference, and robot execution components can operate together on the physical platform. The result is an implementation claim; statistical performance claims require the repeated-trial evidence presented in Chapter~\ref{chap:validation}.
