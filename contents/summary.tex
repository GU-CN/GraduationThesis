\chapter{Discussion and conclusions}\label{chap:discussion}

\section{Discussion}

This project developed a bimanual industrial manipulation system for representative 3C assembly operations. The final system integrates a dual-Franka Research~3 platform, GELLO-based demonstration collection, packet-associated multi-view observations, bilateral proprioceptive-state recording, task-compatible end effectors and fixtures, and a unified $\pi_{0.5}$ VLA policy deployment workflow.

The selected checkpoint executed all three task configurations on the physical dual-arm workcell. Across 75 trials, the system completed 59 trials successfully, giving an overall success rate of 78.7\%. The memory-module task succeeded in 25 of 25 trials (100\%), the ring task succeeded in 21 of 25 trials (84\%), and the plug-insertion task succeeded in 13 of 25 trials (52\%). Independent 25-experiment test sets produced ES1 planning success of 92\%, ES3 component detection of 96\%, ES5 failure detection of 92\%, and ES6 automatic recovery of 92\%. ES5 and ES6 were evaluated on two separate sets of 25 experiments. All 59 completed tasks satisfied the ES4 positional tolerance.

The two FR3 arms, bilateral GELLO demonstration interface, three-camera observation, 16-dimensional state and action representation, LeRobot data pipeline, and unified $\pi_{0.5}$ checkpoint operated together in the physical trials. The 100\% success rate in the memory-module task indicates that its demonstrated division of work---left-arm stabilization followed by right-arm extraction and placement---was effective for the evaluated motherboard configuration.

The ring task succeeded in 21 of 25 manually reset trials. Three of the four failures occurred before secure transport: two were caused by the right gripper approaching away from the handle, and one occurred when the ring base obstructed the handle approach. Only one failure occurred during placement over the can. Handle acquisition is therefore the main area for improvement. Improved wrist-camera visibility, additional demonstrations near displaced handle configurations, and a more repeatable pre-grasp approach would directly address the observed failure pattern.

The plug-insertion task is the least successful and the clearest remaining engineering bottleneck. Two of its twelve failures occurred because the left gripper did not acquire or retain the plug, while ten failures (83.3\% of socket failures) occurred after grasping because the two pins were not aligned accurately enough with the two socket holes. The system can complete the bimanual sequence of repositioning the socket box and acquiring the plug, but it needs greater local precision for reliable insertion. Likely contributors include residual socket-box pose variation, plug-pose variation within the compliant left gripper, and limited visual information at the contact point.

The first priority for further development is to improve plug alignment and insertion through a more constrained fixture or lead-in geometry, a clearer close-range view, and more targeted demonstrations. The ring task would benefit from additional demonstrations and pre-grasp alignment around difficult handle poses. Safe-space estimation and broader data coverage are the next system-level improvements.


\section{Conclusions}

The thesis presents a complete engineering process for VLA-oriented bimanual industrial manipulation. Starting from customer requirements, the project defined measurable engineering specifications, generated modular solution concepts, selected a dual-FR3 and GELLO-based architecture, and implemented a data-driven manipulation pipeline using packet-associated multi-view observations and one jointly trained $\pi_{0.5}$ checkpoint. The final controller receives three RGB observations, a 16-dimensional bilateral robot state, and a language instruction, and generates coordinated actions for both robot arms and grippers.

The completed system demonstrates unified multi-task bimanual control on the physical dual-FR3 workcell using one shared checkpoint and three fixed scene--instruction configurations. The same unified checkpoint was evaluated in 25 trials for each of the three tasks, giving 75 physical trials in total. The ring-over-can task achieved an 84\% success rate, the plug-insertion task achieved 52\%, and the memory-module task achieved 100\%. Across all trials, the observed overall success rate was 78.7\%. Because scene and instruction were not varied independently, these trials do not isolate language grounding or establish robustness to instruction variation.

The overall result exceeded the 70\% task-execution target. The independent ES1, ES3, ES5, and ES6 test sets produced 92\%, 96\%, 92\%, and 92\%, respectively, and all 59 completed tasks met the ES4 positional tolerance. The ring and memory-module tasks exceeded the ES2 target, whereas the plug-insertion task did not. The final result establishes a working proof of concept while identifying plug alignment and insertion as the principal remaining performance limitation.

\chapter{Future Work}\label{chap:future-work}

The completed prototype provides a basis for improving manipulation accuracy,
workspace safety, data coverage, and task generalization.

\section{Improve plug alignment and insertion}

The first priority is the plug-insertion task, which achieved 52\% success.
The next design iteration should improve plug-grasp repeatability, reduce motion
of the socket box, and add a passive lead-in feature or alignment fixture. A
clearer wrist-camera view of the plug pins and socket openings would also help
the policy approach the contact region more consistently. Targeted
demonstrations should focus on the alignment and final insertion stages where
most failures occurred.

The revised task should be evaluated with the same completion criterion and 25
physical trials so that its success rate can be compared directly with the
current result.

\section{Improve safe-space estimation}

The current controller already includes automatic recovery and fixed workspace
checks. A useful next step is to estimate the available safe space from the
packet-associated multi-view observations before and during execution. The
estimated region should account for task fixtures, both robot arms, and the
objects currently present in the workcell.

This estimate can be used to update motion boundaries when fixture placement or
object position changes. Validation should test whether the estimated safe
region prevents the two arms from entering occupied areas while preserving
enough workspace to complete all three tasks.

\section{Expand data coverage and model evaluation}

Additional demonstrations should cover more object positions, grasp poses,
lighting conditions, and fixture placements. Data collection should emphasize
the handle configurations that caused ring-grasp failures and the alignment
conditions that caused plug-insertion failures while keeping the three tasks
balanced during training.

Model revisions should use the same held-out episodes and physical completion
criteria so that improvements can be compared with the current checkpoint and
the 75-trial baseline.

\section{Improve language conditioning and task generalization}

Future experiments should test paraphrases and reordered versions of the three
task instructions while keeping the physical task unchanged. The scene and
instruction can then be varied independently to determine whether the unified
checkpoint selects the requested task consistently.

After the existing tasks remain stable under instruction variation, the same
training pipeline can be extended to additional bimanual assembly operations
that use the D435 overhead view, two D405 wrist views, and the established
16-dimensional bilateral action interface.

\section{Preserve the deployment package}

Future model revisions should preserve the dataset version, training
configuration, selected checkpoint, camera feature mapping, task instructions,
and robot action convention as one versioned deployment package. This keeps the
training and physical evaluation settings aligned across project iterations.

The recommended order is to improve plug insertion first, evaluate safe-space
estimation second, and then expand object, scene, and instruction variation.
